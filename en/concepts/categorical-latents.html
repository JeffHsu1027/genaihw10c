Concept

Categorical latents

discrete latents, categorical latent variables, discrete representations · 2 sources

One-line definition

A latent representation made of several categorical variables, each taking one of a fixed set of classes, instead of continuous Gaussian variables.

Explanation

A Gaussian latent describes a state with continuous numbers and one smooth bump of uncertainty. A vector of categorical variables describes it with discrete choices, so it can express several clearly separate possibilities and abrupt changes. Sampling a category is not differentiable, so training usually relies on the straight-through estimator: the forward pass uses the sampled one-hot vector, while the backward pass uses the gradient of the probabilities as if no sampling had happened. This gradient is biased but has low variance.

Explainer: It is like describing a scene with a set of multiple-choice answers rather than with a slider for each property.

What each source says

Mastering Atari with Discrete World Models (2021)

DreamerV2 represents the stochastic state as 32 categorical variables with 32 classes each, so a flattened sample is a sparse binary vector of length 1024 with 32 active bits (p.9). The categoricals are trained with straight-through gradients, which take three lines with automatic differentiation (p.3, Alg. 1). Categorical latents outperform Gaussian latents on 42 of 55 Atari games, lose on 8, and tie on 5 (p.9). The authors do not know why and offer hypotheses: a categorical prior can fit a mixture of posteriors exactly, the sparsity may help generalization, categoricals may be easier to optimize, and they may suit non-smooth events such as entering a new room (p.9). Using many binary latents instead was worse (p.18).

Mastering Diverse Domains through World Models (2023)

DreamerV3 keeps representations sampled from a vector of softmax distributions with straight-through gradients (p.4). After observing occasional spikes in the KL losses, it mixes each categorical distribution with 1% uniform noise so it can never become deterministic (p.5). The same mixing is used for the actor, to avoid zero probabilities and infinite log probabilities (p.18). The number of codes per latent grows with the model size (p.20).

Comparing the sources

  • DreamerV2 introduces categorical latents and shows their benefit on Atari (p.9).
  • DreamerV3 keeps them across all domains and adds uniform mixing for stability (p.5). DreamerV3 builds on DreamerV2; this summary is this Wiki's own.

Related concepts

  • Recurrent state-space model: DreamerV2 uses categorical latents for the stochastic part of the RSSM.
  • KL balancing: the other main change to the DreamerV2 world model.
  • Variational autoencoder: the usual alternative with Gaussian latents and reparameterization gradients.
  • Vector quantization: another way to make latents discrete, by snapping each vector to its nearest codebook entry.
