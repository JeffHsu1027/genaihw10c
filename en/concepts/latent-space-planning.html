Concept

Latent space planning

planning in latent space, online planning, model-predictive control, MPC, CEM planning · 1 source

One-line definition

Choosing actions by searching over candidate action sequences inside a learned latent dynamics model and scoring them by predicted rewards, without generating observations.

Explanation

At each step, the agent infers its current latent state, imagines the outcome of many candidate action sequences with the model, and picks the sequence with the highest predicted return. In model-predictive control, only the first action is executed and the search is repeated after the next observation, so the plan can adapt to what actually happens. The cross entropy method is a common search procedure: sample sequences, keep the best ones, refit the sampling distribution to them, and repeat. Planning in the latent space is fast because no images need to be produced.

Explainer: It is like a chess player who looks a few moves ahead before every move, then thinks again after seeing the opponent's reply.

What each source says

Learning Latent Dynamics for Planning from Pixels (2019)

PlaNet plans with CEM inside its model and replans at every step, as in model-predictive control (p.3). After each new observation the search starts again from a zero-mean, unit-variance belief over action sequences, to avoid local optima (p.3). It scores each candidate with a single sampled trajectory, which leaves more of the compute budget for evaluating many different sequences (p.3). The settings are a horizon of 12, 10 iterations, 1000 candidates, and refitting to the best 100 (p.12). Iterative refinement with CEM beats choosing the best of 1000 random sequences on all tasks (p.6, Fig. 5). With the true simulator on cheetah, a horizon of 6 is too short and the best horizon is near 8, while much longer horizons hurt because the search space grows (p.20, Fig. 12). Because PlaNet has no value function, rewards beyond the horizon are ignored, and the authors suggest learning one (p.8).

Related concepts

  • Model-based reinforcement learning: planning is one way to use a learned model.
  • Recurrent state-space model: the model PlaNet plans with.
  • Learning in imagination: the other way to use a model; it trains a policy on imagined experience ahead of time, while planning searches at decision time.
